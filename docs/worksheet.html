Apply the framework

Your task through five rungs

The framework only becomes real when it is carried by a task you actually do. This worksheet is the instrument for that: pick one recurring task from your own work and rebuild the running-example table for it, rung by rung, as you climb. Everything you type here is saved in your browser.

Picking the task

Good candidates share three properties:

  1. Recurring — weekly or monthly, so rungs 2+ have something to compound on. One-off tasks never generate the friction that motivates the climb.
  2. Has real inputs and outputs — information comes from somewhere and the result goes somewhere. A task that never touches the world can't cross rung 3.
  3. Yours — you know what good output looks like, so your judgment has something to grip at every gate.

Examples of the right shape: a weekly status report, a recurring competitive scan, a monthly data summary for another team, triaging a shared inbox, preparing a standing meeting's brief.

The point of the exercise

When the table is full, read the "who fires each action" column top to bottom. The task never changed. The tools stopped changing at rung 3. The only thing that moved, rung after rung, was you — and at every position, the part that stayed yours was the judging. That is the framework, demonstrated on your own work.
