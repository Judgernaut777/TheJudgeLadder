# Your task through five rungs — worksheet

The framework only becomes real when it is carried by a task you actually do. This worksheet is the instrument for that: pick **one recurring task from your own work** and rebuild the running-example table for it, rung by rung, as you climb.

## Picking the task

Good candidates share three properties:

1. **Recurring** — weekly or monthly, so rungs 2+ have something to compound on. One-off tasks never generate the friction that motivates the climb.
2. **Has real inputs and outputs** — information comes from somewhere (documents, sites, messages) and the result goes somewhere (an email, a report, a decision). A task that never touches the world can't cross rung 3.
3. **Yours** — you know what good output looks like, so your judgment has something to grip at every gate.

Examples of the right shape: a weekly status report, a recurring competitive scan, a monthly data summary for another team, triaging a shared inbox, preparing a standing meeting's brief. ## Gate check

Before filling a row, confirm the gate below the previous one was true of you. The gates are restraints — each one is something you *hold onto*, not something you achieve and discard.

- [ ] **After rung 1:** I verify factual output by instinct — not-checking now feels wrong.
- [ ] **After rung 2:** My refinements accumulate in saved templates — and I can point at exactly which parts of my routine are judgment and which are copy-paste transport.
- [ ] **After rung 3:** I approved real actions on the real world — and I can state, for every tool involved, where my data goes.
- [ ] **After rung 4:** I handed over the goal and stayed out of the loop — and my review at the gate is real (I could describe the last output I approved).
- [ ] **After rung 5:** I can read this system's guardrails and audit trail well enough to catch drift — whether or not I built any of it.

## Prompts to answer as you climb

**At rung 2:** Which steps of my routine contain judgment, and which are mechanical transport? (Write the two lists. The second list is what rung 3 exists to absorb.)

**At rung 3:** What tools did this task actually need? Where does my data go through each one? What would an injection-shaped anomaly look like *in this task specifically*?

**At rung 4:** Write the handoff: goal, constraints, exclusions, escalation cases. Where did I place the approval gate, and what irreversible action is it guarding?

**At rung 5** *(on paper is enough — building is optional)*: How would this task decompose into single-purpose agents? What guardrails would I write? What would drift look like across a month of runs, and what record would I need in order to notice it?

## The point of the exercise

When the table is full, read the "who fires each action" column top to bottom. The task never changed. The tools stopped changing at rung 3. The only thing that moved, rung after rung, was you — and at every position, the part that stayed yours was the judging. That is the framework, demonstrated on your own work.
